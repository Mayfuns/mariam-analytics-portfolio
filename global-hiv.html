The public-health question

The project was built to make long-term global HIV trends easier to understand and to show whether the burden of disease has improved over time.

The analysis focuses on a practical question: how have infections, HIV-related deaths, treatment coverage and the number of people living with HIV changed across the reporting period?

Analytical approach

The data was structured for year-over-year and long-term trend analysis, with measures created to compare headline public-health indicators across time. Power Query supported preparation and consistency, while Power BI and DAX were used to build reusable metrics and an interactive reporting view.

Treatment CoverageART coverage and related indicators showing progress in access to treatment.

Key findings

  • New HIV infections fell by 59.02% between 2000 and 2024.
  • HIV-related deaths fell by 65.50% across the same period.
  • The long-term direction shows substantial improvement in major burden and mortality indicators, while the continuing number of people living with HIV keeps treatment access and sustained care central to the analysis.
  • The dashboard brings burden, mortality and treatment indicators together so progress can be evaluated in context rather than through a single headline metric.

Decision-support perspective

The dashboard is designed to support public-health interpretation rather than simply display historical figures. Declines in new infections and deaths are important signals of progress, but they are most useful when reviewed alongside treatment coverage and the size of the population living with HIV.

This combined view helps identify where progress has been strongest and where continued prevention, treatment access and monitoring remain important.
