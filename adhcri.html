The challenge

ASEAN health data was spread across multiple indicators and files covering mortality, life expectancy, disease burden, nutrition, immunisation, healthcare expenditure and workforce capacity. The analytical challenge was to make those datasets comparable without mixing measures that use different units.

The project asked: which ASEAN countries face the greatest public-health risks, and where should decision-makers investigate investment and intervention priorities?

My contribution

As part of Team HealthSync, my contribution focused on the analytics workflow: understanding the source data, cleaning and standardising files, reshaping year columns into a long format, supporting the Power BI model and DAX logic, and helping turn the analysis into a clear dashboard and report.

Three-page dashboard

The report was designed as three connected analytical views with Country and Year filtering.

Dashboard page 01ASEAN Public Health Overview
Purpose: Compare broad health outcomes and mortality patterns.
Indicators: Life expectancy, maternal mortality, under-5 mortality, infant mortality and crude death rate.
Dashboard page 02Disease, Nutrition & Child Health Risk
Purpose: Identify overlapping public-health vulnerability.
Indicators: Undernourishment, underweight children, malaria, TB, HIV prevalence and under-5 mortality.
Dashboard page 03Healthcare Capacity & Investment
Purpose: Compare prevention capacity, workforce strength and investment gaps.
Indicators: Government health expenditure, physician density, nurses/midwives, pharmaceutical workers, DPT and measles immunisation.

Data preparation & modelling

Power Query was used to standardise country names, clean text fields, correct data types, reshape year columns, add indicator labels and append multiple health files into a single fact table.

The model used a star-schema approach built around Fact_Health_Indicators with country, year and indicator dimensions. This was especially important because the health indicators used different units and could not be meaningfully averaged together without context.

Key findings

Life expectancy gapsSingapore, Brunei Darussalam, Malaysia, Thailand and Viet Nam showed stronger average life expectancy, while Cambodia, Lao PDR and Myanmar were lower in the analysed historical data.
Child & maternal health inequalityLao PDR, Cambodia and Myanmar showed higher under-5 and maternal mortality indicators than many other countries in the dataset.
Priority modelThe hackathon model placed Lao PDR, Cambodia and Myanmar in the high-priority group for closer investigation.
Regional decision supportThe combined view connected outcomes with disease, nutrition, prevention, investment and workforce capacity rather than treating them as separate datasets.
Important: the data is historical and the hackathon priority model is not an official public-health ranking or live 2026 surveillance system.

Decision-support perspective

The value of ADHCRI is not a single score. It is the ability to bring fragmented indicators together so policymakers and analysts can see where mortality, disease burden, nutrition risk and system capacity overlap, then investigate those areas more deeply.

The project demonstrates healthcare analytics, Power Query transformation, DAX, data modelling, dashboard design and public-health storytelling within a time-limited hackathon environment.
