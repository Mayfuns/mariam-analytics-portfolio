The business question

Lumina brings together healthcare utilisation, patient outcomes and financial activity to answer a practical question: how can operational and financial data be queried together to expose patterns that matter for service performance?

The project focuses on turning relational healthcare data into decision-ready summaries rather than treating activity, outcomes and finance as separate reporting streams.

Analytical approach

The analysis was developed in PostgreSQL using joins across related tables, grouped summaries, conditional logic and date-based calculations. Queries were designed to move from simple descriptive questions into more useful comparisons across services, patients, treatments and financial activity.

SQL techniques demonstrated

  • JOIN operations to connect patient, admission, treatment and financial information across the relational model.
  • GROUP BY and HAVING to create service-level and category-level summaries and filter aggregated results.
  • CASE expressions and conditional aggregation to build meaningful analytical categories and comparative measures.
  • Subqueries to support multi-stage analysis and isolate more complex questions.
  • Date functions to examine timing, length of stay and 30-day readmission behaviour.

Decision-support perspective

The strength of this project is the relationship between operational and financial questions. By querying admissions, outcomes, treatment costs, insurance and revenue within the same analytical workflow, the project demonstrates how SQL can support a more complete view of healthcare performance.

Rather than producing disconnected totals, the analysis is structured to help identify where service activity, patient outcomes and financial performance may need deeper investigation.
