The business question

Talent Pulse needed a clearer view of employee attrition across departments, age groups, tenure and other workforce segments.

The analysis was structured around a practical question: where is attrition concentrated, which employee groups are most exposed to retention risk, and where should leaders focus their attention?

Analytical approach

The project used Tableau for interactive visual analysis and Excel for structured data preparation and validation. Attrition was analysed proportionally across workforce groups to avoid relying only on raw exit counts.

What the analysis covers

Department RiskAttrition rates compared across business functions to identify where employee loss is disproportionately high.
Tenure PatternsEmployee exits examined by years at the company, with particular attention to early-tenure risk.
Age SegmentationAttrition patterns assessed across age groups to reveal workforce segments requiring closer attention.
Retention SignalsMultiple workforce dimensions brought together to support more targeted retention decisions.

Key findings

  • Overall attrition was 16.4%, with 243 exits across a workforce of 1,485 employees.
  • Sales recorded a 20.54% attrition rate, making it one of the clearest department-level pressure points.
  • Employees aged 18–26 recorded 34.57% attrition, indicating elevated turnover among younger employees.
  • 30.43% attrition occurred within the first two years, highlighting early tenure as a major retention risk period.

Decision-support perspective

The analysis helps move the conversation beyond a single organisation-wide attrition rate. By showing where turnover is concentrated, leaders can distinguish broad workforce issues from specific department, age or tenure-related patterns.

This supports more targeted actions such as strengthening onboarding, reviewing early-career support and investigating retention challenges within high-risk departments.
