The business question

MotivaAutos asks how customer, vehicle and order data can be combined to understand who is buying, what is selling, where revenue is coming from and how commercial performance changes over time.

The focus is not simply writing queries, but turning relational tables into reproducible answers that a commercial team can use for customer, product and sales decisions.

From relational data to commercial insight

The project follows a practical SQL workflow: understand the customer, car and order tables, join the relationships, calculate commercial measures, segment customers and products, then interpret the outputs around business questions.

What the analysis covers

Customers & OrdersCustomer order counts, high-spending customers and customer-level revenue using relational joins and grouped analysis.
Vehicle Sales PerformanceRevenue and quantity sold by vehicle, plus pricing and product-category analysis.
Customer SegmentationAge groups and revenue-based loyalty tiers created with CASE expressions.
Sales TrendsYearly and monthly revenue patterns using PostgreSQL date extraction and aggregation.

SQL capabilities demonstrated

  • JOINs to connect customers, cars and orders into a usable commercial view.
  • GROUP BY, HAVING and aggregate functions to calculate revenue, quantity sold, order counts and customer spend.
  • CASE expressions to create age, vehicle-price and loyalty segments.
  • Subqueries to compare customer spend against overall averages and answer multi-stage questions.
  • Date functions to analyse annual and monthly sales trends.
  • COALESCE, CAST and formatting to make query outputs clearer and more business-readable.

Decision-support perspective

MotivaAutos shows SQL as more than a technical extraction tool. The queries are used to create a reliable analytical layer between raw operational data and the questions a business stakeholder may want answered.

This is the same principle used in larger BI workflows: build trustworthy logic first, then communicate the result clearly.
