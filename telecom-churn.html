The business question

The analysis starts with a simple retention question: which customer behaviours and service signals are most strongly associated with churn, and which groups should the telecom company prioritise for retention?

The goal is not to produce a black-box churn prediction. It is to create an explainable analytical view that a customer-retention team can understand and act on.

Data quality & preparation

The project uses 3,150 telecom customer records. Before analysis, the dataset was profiled for missing values, data types, duplicate profiles and coded categorical fields. Column names were standardised and readable labels were created for complaint, tariff, customer status and churn.

No missing valuesThe dataset was complete across the analysed fields.
Duplicate profiles retainedIdentical feature rows were not automatically deleted because the source contains no unique customer identifier.
Coded fields mappedNumeric categories were converted into readable business labels without overwriting the original source fields.
Transparent derivationsTenure, call-activity and SMS-activity bands were created explicitly in Pandas.

Analytical approach

The analysis moves from the overall churn rate into progressively more specific behavioural segments, then combines those signals in a transparent retention-priority heuristic.

Key findings

  • Overall churn was 15.7%, with 495 churners across 3,150 customers.
  • Customers who complained churned at 83.0%, compared with 10.1% among customers without a complaint.
  • Non-active customers recorded 47.3% churn, compared with 5.3% among active customers.
  • Pay-as-you-go customers recorded 16.8% churn, versus 2.4% for contractual customers.
  • Customers with no call activity churned at 52.6%, while customers with 76+ calls recorded only 3.3% churn.
  • Customers sending 101+ SMS recorded just 0.7% churn, reinforcing the broader engagement pattern.

Call engagement

Churn falls sharply as call activity increases, making customer engagement one of the strongest behavioural themes in the project.

No-call customers recorded the highest churn, while highly active callers recorded very low churn.

Subscription length

Customers in their first 12 months recorded the highest churn among the tenure bands used in the analysis.

Early-tenure customers represent a useful segment for proactive retention attention.

Retention-priority segmentation

A transparent business-rule score was created from complaint, activity, tenure and tariff signals. It is a prioritisation heuristic, not a machine-learning probability.

High-priority customers recorded 56.6% observed churn, Medium 28.0%, and Low 1.8%.

Decision-support perspective

The combined evidence points toward service friction and weak engagement as the central retention story. Complaint recovery, re-engagement of inactive and low-usage customers, first-year retention and pay-as-you-go strategy are more actionable than treating churn as one organisation-wide percentage.

The project deliberately avoids presenting Customer Value as monetary revenue and states that the retention score must be validated on future outcomes before being used for automated customer decisions.

Recommendations

  • Prioritise complaint recovery and investigate complaint-resolution quality and timing.
  • Trigger re-engagement workflows for inactive and low-usage customers.
  • Strengthen first-year retention through onboarding and early engagement checkpoints.
  • Review pay-as-you-go retention and test appropriate contractual or loyalty offers.
  • Validate the retention-priority heuristic prospectively before operational automation.
